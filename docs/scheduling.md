# Scheduling policy

- A one-time reminder stores a resolved instant. Changing the Mac's time zone changes how that instant is displayed, not when it fires.
- A recurring reminder is anchored to the IANA time zone, local date, and wall-clock time chosen when it is created or edited. Moving the Mac to another time zone does not change the series' zone; the interface should show that zone.
- When creating or editing, local times that are nonexistent or ambiguous because of daylight-saving changes are rejected with an explanation rather than silently choosing an instant. For future daily or weekly dates where the chosen wall time is nonexistent or ambiguous, that occurrence is skipped; the next valid date is used. Explicitly selected dates with ambiguous or nonexistent times are rejected when saved. The app cannot alert while the Mac is asleep or powered off or while Sela is not running.
- A range's end date is inclusive in the series' time zone. Explicitly selected dates share a time and are normalized and de-duplicated.
- After downtime or sleep, at most one missed occurrence per series is surfaced. Once it is handled, the next occurrence is the first valid scheduled time in the future. Snoozing changes only the current occurrence's delivery time, not the series schedule.
- Future occurrences are strictly later than the query instant: equality consumes an occurrence, including the final occurrence on an inclusive end date. An exhausted recurrence remains completed after restart and retains its recorded occurrence history.
- Calendar validation handles offset changes of any size, not only one-hour DST changes. This includes Lord Howe's half-hour transitions and Apia's skipped calendar date.
- Relative timers and snoozes are persisted as Unix-millisecond deadlines, not elapsed monotonic durations. Their deadline survives restart, but moving the system clock forward can make them due sooner and moving it backward can delay them. Sleep and downtime count toward the deadline; Sela does not wake a sleeping Mac. This is the existing timer behavior, not a guarantee of elapsed-time precision.
- Transient delivery spacing is different: the five-second pause after accepted native delivery and three-second pause after failed delivery use a process-local monotonic clock. System-clock changes cannot extend or bypass those pauses, and restarting the app resets them.
- Activities can rename a pending reminder without changing its timing, or atomically replace its title and timing with a new relative timer or **No timer**. Replacing timing removes any calendar schedule and current snooze but retains recorded occurrence history. Stale edits are rejected using the occurrence revision; unsaved drafts remain available until the user explicitly reloads the latest version.
- Sela stores at most 10,000 reminder rows, including completed and cancelled rows, and accepts at most 10,000 explicitly selected dates per schedule. History is not silently truncated. **Delete resolved history** requires explicit confirmation and deletes only completed/cancelled rows; pending reminders and their occurrence histories remain untouched. This restores capacity for new reminders. Durable backups can still retain previously saved history.
- Previous can reopen a resolved reminder with its original timing and schedule; if that time is overdue it may alert immediately. Reopening an individual completed recurring occurrence atomically removes that history entry and creates a separate one-time reminder, without changing the series' next occurrence. Stale or repeated recovery requests are rejected.
